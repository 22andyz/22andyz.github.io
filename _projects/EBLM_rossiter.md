summary: "Joint Photometric Transit and RV fits of the Rossiter-McLaughlin Effect to calculate true obliquities of short period eclipsing stellar binaries."

# date: "Spring 2025"

Short period or tight stellar binaries are physically anomalous systems in terms of their dynamical evolutionary history. Collapsing protostellar clouds typically contain far too much angular momentum for short period stars to form, yet thousands of binary stars have been found separated by less than 1 AU. Thus, it has long been suspected that binaries form through migration via mechanisms like the EKL effect rather than in situ. These migratory mechanisms perturb the system in ways that alter the spin and orbital axes of the stars, which can be measured long after the migration has completed. 

As part of the Eclipsing Binary Low Mass (EBLM) project, we used high precision radial velocity data of six close in, short period binary star systems along with TESS photometry of their transits to derive the 3D spin-orbit axis misalignment, or true obliquity, of the primaries. We use these measurements to test the evolutionary history of these tight binaries, finding a majority are close to spin-orbit alignment, having true obliquities between 5˚ and 20˚. This contrasts against a far more diverse range of misalignments from similar work done on hot Jupiters, suggesting that tidal realignment of binary systems is more effective for smaller mass ratios.

THese measurements have greatly expanded the existing sample of sky-projected obliquity measurements of eclipsing binaries from 7 to 13, and are presently the only measurements for short period EBs. These results have been published as the 16th and 18th EBLM papers respectively.
